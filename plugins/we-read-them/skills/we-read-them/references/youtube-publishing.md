# We Read Them YouTube playbook

The original series research dates to November 22, 2026. Discovery, upload metadata and catalog verification were checked against YouTube Help on November 24, 2026. Record the actual release and edit dates for each video separately. Historical creator opinions and third-party studies are labeled below.

---

## Before posting or refreshing a video

Start the [publication record](../templates/youtube-publication.md) with the channel, video ID, and requested action. For a catalog refresh, capture the current Studio details and public presentation first. Edit the existing video only within the authorized scope.

1. For a new upload, stage it privately, allow time for processing and applicable checks, and use the producer’s authorized release date, time and time zone. For a refresh, record the prior title and save the prior custom thumbnail if available.
2. Write the audience’s question and the film’s answer. Choose one or two specific phrases for the title and the first description sentences (§2). Check the opening against the promised story.
3. Preview the title with each [Club-branded thumbnail](thumbnail-workshop.md) at feed size. Select a clear default. Keep alternative treatments for an eligible Studio test when enough viewers are seeing the video (§5).
4. Write unique opening sentences. Add useful chapter labels and timestamps from the final cut, source links and any required image credit. Link a closely related public video or playlist when there is a genuine next question. Open each destination. Use tags for useful spelling variants and optional relevant hashtags (§2).
5. Check the spoken language and the selected original-language field. Supply or review the timed captions, especially proper names, quotations and terms. Set audience and AI disclosures from the actual material (§7). Record the caption type and language visible in the player. [Video language](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6304325) · [Captions](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2734796)
6. Check Studio’s channel feature eligibility for the specific action. Custom thumbnails use Intermediate access. Advanced access can come from sufficient channel history or, where offered, ID or video verification. Chapter navigation, A/B testing, and a Short’s related-video link each have eligibility conditions. Record an unavailable feature and continue the applicable checks. [Feature access](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9890437) · [Chapters](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9884579) · [A/B tests](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400)
7. Use an end screen, card, related Short link or playlist when it offers a meaningful next watch. For a Shorts release, use the approved schedule and check claims on Shorts over 60 seconds (§4). Channel layout, monetization setup and extra promotional posts are separate tasks.
8. After an authorized change, reopen the saved Studio details and the public watch page by the exact ID. Open the expanded description and check the related video, a chapter timestamp and an external source separately. YouTube requires Advanced access for clickable external URLs in long-form descriptions. If sources appear as plain text, keep their names and document titles readable and record what viewers see. [YouTube link eligibility](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13748639)
9. Verify the actual public cover at feed size against the selected artwork and log any mismatch alongside the time checked. Use §5 for analytics when impressions accumulate.

## 1. What drives recommendations now, and what changes for a new channel

**YouTube's own model: three buckets per video.** The Help Center splits performance into **Appeal** (did people choose it, ignore it, or click "not interested"), **Engagement** ("do they stick around?"), and **Satisfaction** ("Did viewers enjoy the video?").
https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16559650

- The system has two stated goals: help each viewer find videos and "maximize long-term viewer satisfaction". It ranks on viewer personalization (watch history, interest affinity) plus content performance. https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16533387
- For long-form, the ranking inputs YouTube names are click, average view duration, average percentage viewed, and satisfaction measured "using a variety of signals such as user surveys and likes". Creator Insider FAQ: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fApg7tzlLjY
- Satisfaction surveys: viewers rate videos 1-5 stars, and only 4-5 star ratings count as "valued watchtime". A model predicts the rating for everyone who wasn't surveyed. https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/on-youtubes-recommendation-system
- Other signals YouTube lists include likes, dislikes, "Not interested" and "Don't recommend channel". It also says videos made with AI "can be recommended as broadly as videos made without AI". https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16089387
- There is no single magic metric. YouTube says weights differ by viewer and device, so compare a video with your own past videos, not with a benchmark. Todd Beaupré (head of growth and discovery), Creator Insider, Sept 1, 2026: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rHLjxrbXmmY

**What changes for a brand-new channel (same interview, 31:47):**
- Nobody has us in their watch history, so YouTube uses "parts of the system that don't depend on" a viewer's history with the channel. It matches the video's topic to viewers who like that topic, then grows or stops depending on how that first group responds. Practical effect: **the topic has to be obvious** from title, thumbnail and description, and it should be a topic people already want. Famous cases are an advantage here.
- **There is no subscriber "test."** Even good channels see about 10% or less click-through in the subscription feed (37:04). Subscribers are not a gate the video must pass.
- **Don't uncheck "notify subscribers."** Beaupré: "I wouldn't advise people to turn off notifying subscribers" (34:42). Homepage ranking learns from homepage behavior. Traffic from embeds, ads or links from friends gets little weight: "it doesn't make sense for us to weight that information very much" (34:29). Sharing links won't hurt us. Buying ads won't "kick-start" the homepage either.
- A slow first hour is not a verdict. YouTube keeps testing a video for weeks (chapter at 3:06 of the same video).
- Each video is judged mostly on its own. YouTube adds a caveat: "frequently sharing content that doesn't resonate, can, over time, affect your channel's overall performance." https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16533387
- Give new visitors something to binge. Rene Ritchie (Creator Liaison) said in an interview to put about five videos live so someone discovering the channel can keep watching: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ng2miYe_oDI. Our 11 Shorts per case plus the long-form does this from case 1.

**[Opinion: Paddy Galloway]** "Subscriber counts don't matter in 2026. Average views per video = the success metric." He spends at least 25% of production time on titles, thumbnails, ideas and intros. https://www.linkedin.com/posts/paddy-galloway-459b8913a_i-just-turned-30-ive-dedicated-over-50-activity-7460328048635191296-IFmy

## 2. Titles, descriptions, tags, hashtags, chapters

Start with one question someone would type and one reason a person scrolling Home would stop. Search matches the query to the title, description and actual video and considers engagement for that search. Home and Suggested personalize recommendations. The same truthful title and image should identify the subject for a searcher and raise a specific question for a browser. In the video's Reach report, record relevant search terms that actually brought viewers. With no query report yet, label your wording an editorial choice. [YouTube discovery FAQ](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805) · [Video Reach reports](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314355)

| Field | Publishing check |
|---|---|
| Title | Put the main subject and a concrete promise early. Choose one or two topic words or phrases people use, then work them naturally into the title and description. Test the pair against the film’s opening. [Title and thumbnail tips](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300) · [Description tips](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12948449) |
| Description | Write a unique first sentence naming the subject and the question. Use the second to identify what the film shows or why the answer matters. Preview the collapsed description on a narrow screen. Move series boilerplate, source credits and related viewing below those sentences. [Description tips](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12948449) |
| Tags | Tags have little discovery impact. Put genuine alternate spellings or commonly mistyped names in Studio’s tags field if useful. Keep keyword lists out of viewer-facing copy. [YouTube tag guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/146402) |
| Hashtags | Optional. Keep only labels that describe this specific film. YouTube may surface up to three by the title. It ignores all hashtags when there are more than 60. [YouTube hashtag guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6390658) |
| Chapters | Begin at 00:00 with at least three timestamps in ascending order. Every chapter, including the last, must run at least ten seconds. Use plain labels that name the evidence or story at that point. Verify player navigation separately from the timestamp text. [Chapter requirements](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9884579) |

### Long-form description

Write the actual opening before filling the rest. Delete unused lines and placeholders. Use the rights holder’s exact credit wherever required.

```text
{Subject, specific question and a concrete detail from this film.}
{What the records, interview or demonstration help the viewer understand.}

{Production disclosure when applicable.}

Chapters
00:00 {What the opening asks}
{Time} {First finding in plain language}
{Time} {Next finding}
{Continue with the final export’s actual times and labels.}

Watch next
{Title of one closely related public video or playlist} — {working URL}

Sources and credits
{Document or interview, author or issuing body, date and link}
{Asset, creator, license and link as required}

{Optional relevant hashtags}
```

### Shorts description

Write one specific hook for the individual Short. When the full episode answers the next question, set it in Studio’s **Related Video** field if eligible. URLs in Shorts descriptions and comments are plain text. [YouTube link visibility](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13748639) · [Related video for a Short](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14075157)

## 3. Upload timing

**What YouTube says:** "Don't overthink your publish time". Publishing when the audience is active may bring more immediate views, but "publish time is not known to impact a video's long-term performance". The exception is a Premiere or a live stream. https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16533387

Use the requested release time. Private staging gives processing and applicable checks time to complete. Use Studio’s audience activity report when planning a live stream or Premiere. [YouTube performance FAQ](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805)

**What the data studies say** (they measure engagement such as likes and comments, not views, and the times are local):
- **Buffer, 1.8M videos (2026):** long-form is best Sunday 10 a.m. (by a wide margin), then Sunday 9 a.m. and Friday 12 p.m. Mornings from 8-11 a.m. lead. Shorts are best Friday at 4, 6 and 7 p.m., with evenings from 6-11 p.m. leading. Long-form and Shorts peak at "almost opposite" times. https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-youtube. (Buffer's own all-platform summary table lists long-form at "6-10 p.m. (Sun-Tue)", which contradicts that page. That tells you how soft these numbers are: https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-social-media)
- **SocialPilot, 301K videos from 27K channels (2026):** long-form is best uploaded 2-4 p.m., 2-3 hours before a 6-9 p.m. viewing peak, on Wednesday through Friday. Shorts do best 12-2 p.m. or 6-7 p.m., on Friday, Saturday and Thursday. https://www.socialpilot.co/insights/best-time-to-post-on-youtube
- **Niche data:** none of these studies breaks out US true crime or sports. What we do know about the audience is that true crime skews female. In Triton's 2024 podcast report, 67% of true-crime listeners were women: https://finance.yahoo.com/news/triton-digital-releases-2024-u-140000686.html. That's podcast data, not YouTube, but it fits weekend sit-down viewing.

**Default slot: long-form Sunday 10:00 a.m. CT, scheduled. Shorts at 12:00 p.m. and 7:00 p.m. CT.**
Why: it is the top long-form slot in the largest study, and 10 a.m. CT reaches 11 ET and 8 PT. True-crime documentaries are weekend, sit-down, often TV viewing. The Shorts slots sit in both studies' evening and midday windows. Most important, YouTube says the clock doesn't affect long-term results. So the real value is a **fixed** slot, which keeps every case's first 48 hours comparable with the others. Don't chase a better hour.

## 4. Shorts and long-form together

When a numbered chapter becomes a standalone Short, put the viewer’s question in the first spoken sentence and keep the rank on screen. Name the person or case before an unfamiliar viewer needs it. Check any tightened chronology against the dated sources. Two events three days apart establish the dates of those events, while a request or decision leading up to them can have a different date. Compare the first phone-size frame and the exact final spoken phrase with the new captions.

Test the native Related video control on the target channel before promising a link. When Studio requires owner verification and the producer chooses to keep the release date, put the exact public title of the full film and its watch URL in the description. Record the Short ID, parent ID and feature status so the native link can be added to the same upload when eligible. A URL in a Shorts description displays as plain text. [Shorts related videos](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14075157) · [Link behavior](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13748639)

**Do Shorts hurt the long-form? YouTube says no, but don't expect automatic spillover either.**
- The two formats are recommended separately. A 2022 YouTube Q&A said "we separate Shorts and long-form content from watch history", and channels that made Shorts "actually seemed to be growing faster". https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-shorts-algorithm-explained-in-qa-format/459513 (July 2022, older)
- The 2026 view is the same, per viewer: for "long form and shorts on the same channel… we want to show all those things to that viewer". When a viewer only watches one format, YouTube focuses their recommendations on that format. Beaupré, Sept 2026, 26:15: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rHLjxrbXmmY
- **[Study, correlational]** One 2024 academic paper found that creators who mostly made long-form saw long-form views fall after they started Shorts. Creators who mostly made Shorts saw no such drop. https://arxiv.org/html/2402.18208v2
- Takeaway: Shorts bring reach and subscribers. The bridge to the long-form is the **Related Video** link, which we have to set by hand.

**Related Video on Shorts:** you link one video from your channel, and it shows as a clickable link under the channel handle. It is "designed to complement your Short and encourage viewers to watch more of your content", and it requires Advanced features. https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14075157. **Set it on all 11 Shorts, pointing to that case's long-form.**

**Shorts series (new, Made on YouTube, Sept 2026):** put each case's 11 Shorts into a **Shorts series**. Viewers who find an episode see a **"watch series"** button that opens a dedicated feed where they swipe from episode to episode. The playlist "must only contain Shorts", portrait or square, up to 180 s each. Use **Serial** so it plays in order: intro, then #10 down to #1. https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16590581 and https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/made-on-youtube-creators-shorts-series-tv-features. (One creator says Show features need YPP. That's unverified. The Help page lists no requirement. Check whether Studio offers it.)

**How many a day:** YouTube says frequency isn't a lever. Todd Sherman, then head of Shorts: "nothing in our systems… when you hit the five video or ten short threshold per day". https://www.benzinga.com/news/23/08/34020532/youtube-shorts-chief-debunks-popular-myths-no-posting-10-videos-a-day-doesnt-affect-your-performance. Every Short gets its own test.
- **[Opinion: FlowShorts]** Space Shorts 4-6 hours apart so they don't compete for the same audience at the same time. https://www.flowshorts.app/blog/how-many-shorts-per-day
- **[Unconfirmed, one analyst via SEJ]** Since mid-September 2026, Shorts older than about 30 days reportedly get far fewer impressions. If that's true, Shorts are front-loaded, which argues for releasing a case's Shorts within about a week rather than dripping them out over a month. https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-shorts-algorithm-may-now-favor-fresh-over-evergreen/562661

**The originality update (Oct 1, 2026). It matters for us because the Shorts carry news clips.** YouTube is updating Shorts recommendations to cut reach for channels "primarily aggregating or re-uploading other creators' videos without significant changes". https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2026/10/02/youtube-shorts-prioritize-original-content. Ritchie said added value means more than "VO descriptions of what's happening on screen". https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-original-shorts-reposted-clips-reach/591774. What keeps us on the right side: our narration argues a point, the documents are on screen, and the clip is evidence for the argument rather than the content itself. Keep it that way. Never let a reel's narration just describe the clip.

**Copyright on Shorts over 60 s is a hard trap.** "Any Short that is over one minute in duration with an active copyright claim of any type… will be blocked globally." https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877. Five JonBenét reels run over 60 s (intro 75.6, #1 78.2, #2 76.8, #6 65.1, #7 60.9), and so do Canseco's intro (69.3), #2 (73.7) and #8 (69.9). All of them use network news footage. **Rule:** a Short with third-party footage either runs 60 s or less, or must pass the Studio copyright check before it goes public.

**Release plan for one case (Day 0 = long-form day, times CT):**

|---|---|---|---|
| Day 0 (Sun) | Long-form at 10:00 a.m. | Intro Short (6:00 p.m.) | Intro's Related Video points to the long-form |
| Day 1 (Mon) | #10 | #9 | |

| Day 3 (Wed) | #6 | #5 | |

## 5. Pair the title with the thumbnail and the opening

Follow the [thumbnail workshop](thumbnail-workshop.md) on the publication JPEGs or PNGs. It supplies the three treatments, neutral review procedure, exact feed proof command and source-credit handoff for the person posting the video.

Choose three details the finished film can prove. For each pair, name the viewer who would care, write one title, and choose one image that adds information. A search viewer may recognize the player or game. Someone scrolling Home may recognize the feeling of calling home after the biggest win of your life. If the detail only appears late in the countdown, move a truthful glimpse into the opening or use the pair for that chapter’s Short. ### Learn from the published edit

- **Long-form.** When Studio has enough data, compare Home and Suggested click-through rates separately with similar videos at comparable impression volume. Then inspect the first 30 seconds of retention. If people choose the video but leave before they see the promised evidence, replay that stretch and try bringing the witness or document forward in the next opening. A later spike can reflect either interest or confusion. Watch the precise passage before deciding to repeat the approach. [Thumbnail performance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300) · [Audience retention](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415)
- **Search and small samples.** Save the date range, impression count and CTR for each traffic source alongside the available first-30-second retention, and transcribe only search terms shown in Reach. When impressions are sparse or the query/retention report is unavailable, record that state. Wait for comparable data before attributing movement to the edit. [Reach reports](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314355) · [CTR and impression guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154)
- **Shorts.** For feed discovery, read **Stayed to watch** alongside average percentage viewed among the viewers who stayed. If comparatively few stop but those who do finish, try a closer first shot or a more specific opening line on the next Short. [YouTube’s Shorts metrics](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281)
- **Experiments.** Eligible long-form videos can use desktop Studio to test up to three titles, thumbnails, or title-and-thumbnail pairs concurrently. For a thumbnail-only refresh, hold the title fixed. For a pair test, interpret the pair together. Put the preferred default first. YouTube evaluates watch time and can report no clear winner, especially with few impressions. Record the actual result after the test, which can take up to two weeks. [Official A/B test instructions and eligibility](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400)

## 6. End screens, cards, pinned comment, playlists, Shows, trailer, Home tab

- **End screens.** For eligible videos at least 25 seconds long, place a relevant next-watch element in the final 5–20 seconds. Preview placement over the closing evidence and captions. Select a video or playlist that answers the viewer’s next question. [YouTube end screens](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6388789)
- **Cards:** "You can add up to 5 cards to one video." They can link a video, playlist, channel or link. https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6140493. Add one where the narration mentions another case or the full Shorts series.
- **Pinned comment:** requires Advanced features ([9890437](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9890437)). Use: the 2-3 strongest sources plus one question that invites a real answer ("Which of the ten did you already know?"). YouTube counts comments and replies as engagement signals ([16533387](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16533387)).
- **Playlists, now Shows:** a Show is "a collection of high-quality episodes with a clear connection", and Shows "may be eligible to appear in search as well as under 'Recommended shows' and 'Continue watching.'" Best practice is the same intro, the same thumbnail style and the same format, which is us. https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16590581. Make **one Show, "We Read Them" (non-serial, newest first)** holding every long-form, and **one Shorts series per case** (serial). The old "official series playlist" toggle still has a Help page (a verified account is required, and a video can be in only one series): https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6084043. Shows are the current tool, so use Shows.
- **Channel trailer and Home tab:** the trailer plays for people who aren't subscribed. "Once a viewer watches your channel trailer, it won't be shown to them again." Returning subscribers can get a separate Spotlight video. The page holds "up to 12 custom sections". https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/3219384. **Layout for now:** trailer = the newest long-form, until we cut a 60-90 s channel trailer from the series intro. Sections, top to bottom: the Show (all cases), the newest case's Shorts series, Popular, Shorts.
- YouTube's own advice for helping viewers find more of your videos is to build series, use calls to action ("If you liked this, then watch…"), playlists and end screens. https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16533387

## 7. AI narration, disclosure, inauthentic content, monetization

Inspect the assets in the released cut and answer the current **AI use** questions in Studio for that video. YouTube requires disclosure of realistic synthetic scenes that did not happen, meaningful alteration of real events or people, and other listed uses such as cloning someone else’s voice. Its guidance exempts ordinary assistance with scripts, titles and thumbnails. The series can credit the synthetic narrator in the description independently. Record the Studio answer and the reason. [YouTube disclosure guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)

- **Never** use AI to make a victim speak. The harassment policy bans "Content that realistically simulates deceased or abused individuals describing their death or violence experienced." https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2802268. Never clone a real person's voice for the narrator.

**Inauthentic content** (renamed from "repetitious content" on July 15, 2025). YouTube calls it a "minor update". It applies "regardless of how the content was made", and AI channels "remain eligible to monetize". Its examples of mass-produced content include "A channel that uploads narrated stories with only superficial differences between them". https://support.google.com/youtube/thread/356734251
- **Allowed:** the same intro and outro "but the bulk of your content is different", and series where "each video has a distinct storyline, focus, or concept". **Not allowed:** "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production", plus templated storylines or slideshows with little commentary. https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392
- **Reused content (our news clips) uses the same policy page.** Allowed: clips used for review or commentary, and footage where "you add a storyline and commentary". Not allowed: "Content that exclusively features readings of other materials you did not originally create" (the full text continues "like text from websites or news feeds"). **That line is the risk for a channel called "We Read Them."** Reading the record is fine as long as every reel also carries our analysis: the "our take" beat, the contradiction, the opinion. Reviewers look at the videos, the **channel description, titles and descriptions**, so the channel description should state the method ("we read the primary record and tell you what it does and doesn't prove").
- What keeps us eligible: a different case each time with a distinct angle, an argument in every reel, original research (FACTS gate, page-cited documents), news clips kept short and always commented on, and the methods line in every description. A related section of the same policy bars AI personas that give medical, legal or financial *advice* from monetizing. We report the record and don't advise, so keep it that way.

**Monetization thresholds**
- **Now:** 1,000 subscribers **plus** either 4,000 qualified long-form watch hours in 12 months **or** 10M qualified Shorts views in 90 days. Shorts-feed watch time doesn't count toward the 4,000 hours. Channels also need Advanced features, 2-Step Verification, AdSense, and no active strikes. https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851
- **From Feb 1, 2027 (new applicants):** "8,000 qualified watch hours in the last 365 days, or 20M qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days". Channels already in YPP are not affected. Also from Feb 1, 2027, Shorts revenue needs 10M qualified Shorts views per trailing 90 days. https://support.google.com/youtube/thread/451804019
- What that means for us: 4,000 hours at about a 4-minute average view on the long-form is roughly 60,000 long-form views. 8,000 hours is roughly 120,000. Only the long-form counts toward the hours path. Shorts mostly earn the 1,000 subscribers.
- A lower fan-funding tier (500 subscribers) exists according to third parties (https://air.io/en/youtube-glossary/what-is-the-youtube-partner-program-ypp-requirements-benefits). It isn't on the Help page I read. Check Studio > Earn.

## 8. True crime and advertiser-friendly rules (staying green)

The rules cover "all portions of your content", which includes the video, thumbnail, title, description and tags. https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278. They matter once we're in YPP. Ads aren't shared before that.

| Green (full ads) | Yellow (limited ads) | Red (no ads) |
|---|---|---|
| "Reports of nearby homicide events without graphic descriptions of the casualties"; coverage of tragedies "with limited or no display of violent acts or their results"; police on regular duty; dead bodies "fully obscured, blurred" in educational context | "A documentary on a recent homicide featuring descriptive language of the circumstances of death"; dead bodies with obvious injury in documentary settings; "description of graphic details of tragedies" | Focus on "blood, guts, gore… crime scenes or accident photos with little to no context"; the moment of death |
| Non-graphic references to controversial issues in thumbnails | "Non-graphic, non-descriptive, main topic related to child abuse"; graphic depictions in the thumbnail | "Descriptive content related to child abuse as the main topic" |
| Mild words ("hell", "damn"); obscured profanity | "Moderate profanity (like 'shit') in the title or thumbnail"; profanity at the start or through most of the video | Strong profanity ("f*ck") in the title or thumbnail |
| Educational or news drug content | "Non-educational… content focusing on illegal drug consumption (including injection)" | Promoting or instructing drug use |

**What this means for us:**
- **JonBenét (a child victim).** Name the cause of death once, plainly and clinically, then move on. No autopsy, crime-scene or body imagery. No sexual-assault details in narration, title or thumbnail. The case is about the investigation, not the abuse. That keeps it out of "descriptive child abuse as the main topic", which is the no-ads row.
- **Canseco.** The steroid era is news and history (green). Don't build a thumbnail around a needle or an injection close-up.
- **Language.** Let the evidence carry the argument. Use clean titles and thumbnails, and review the opening lines for language that affects ad suitability.
- Once in YPP, rate each video honestly in the ad-suitability questionnaire at upload. The ad-suitability review can take up to 24 hours and is sometimes done by a person (policy note of March 10, 2025, on https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392). That's one more reason to upload Private early.

## 9. Anything else that measurably matters for a faceless research channel

1. **Feature eligibility.** Read the active channel’s Studio settings for each feature the release needs. Save the observed status. When chapter access is pending, viewers may still see timestamps in the description. Verify the separate chapter navigation in the public player. [Feature eligibility](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9890437) · [Video chapters](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9884579)
2. **Copyright on news footage (30-50% of reel runtime).** Upload Private and wait for Checks. A Content ID claim on the long-form can send its revenue to the claimant or block the video in some countries. A claim on a Short over 60 s blocks it globally (§4). Keep each clip short and commented on; that is also what the reused-content and Shorts-originality rules reward.
3. **Never delete and re-upload a flop.** "YouTube might consider that as spam." Make a new, better video instead. https://blog.youtube/culture-and-trends/renes-top-five-on-youtube-april-18-2024-edition. Also: "if your video's already doing well, don't change what's working" ([Creator Insider FAQ](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fApg7tzlLjY)).
4. **Consistent packaging is a named growth factor.** "a consistent title and thumbnail style makes your videos instantly identifiable" ([16533387](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16533387)). Keep the series typography and evidence treatment recognizable across cases.
5. **The first seconds.** YouTube: the intro must deliver on the title and thumbnail's promise, so get to the value right away ([16559650](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16559650)). **[Opinion: Paddy Galloway]** cut everything after the payoff, so the ending feels "slightly too fast, not slightly too slow" (LinkedIn link in §1). Our 2-second hook rule already fits.
6. **Search quality is E-A-T-like.** YouTube looks for channels that show "expertise, authoritativeness, and trustworthiness on a given topic" ([16090438](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16090438)). Sourced descriptions, page citations on screen, and a channel About that states the method are how a faceless channel shows that.
7. **Living people.** Several people in our cases are alive and litigious (the Ramseys sued CBS). Attribute every theory, and keep the record's answer on screen (already a skill rule). YouTube honors privacy and harassment complaints about identifiable people, so the FACTS gate is also our defense on the platform.
8. **More languages, later.** Beaupré: upload translated titles and descriptions, and note that a viewer who finds you in a language can watch more if more of the catalog is in it. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dhYIb72L1hU. We already have ElevenLabs, so a Spanish audio track is cheap once a case proves itself. This is an option, not a now item.
9. **Pre-publish Shorts feedback.** "Get Feedback" checks a Short's hook and pacing before you post. It is US-only, mobile app only (Sept 2026). https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-gives-you-feedback-on-shorts-before-publishing/589088. It's worth one test on a reel.
