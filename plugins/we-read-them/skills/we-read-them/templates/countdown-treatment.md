# Countdown treatment

Use this for the episode’s `film/TREATMENT.md`. Write in the language the narrator and editor will use. Cite the relevant rows in `film/FACTS.md`.

## Opening

**Working title and viewer question** — What did the title lead someone to expect? What do they want to find out?

**Audience and stakes** — Picture one viewer coming to this cold. What might they be choosing, remembering, feeling or trying to understand in their own life? Why would the answer matter to them there?

**First spoken beat and first shot** — Write the opening words verbatim. What are we doing with this subject, why does it matter, and what could the evidence show us? Name the source and the exact choice, action or detail the first shot lets us examine. Leave room to notice it.

**First discovery and cut into number ten** — What small answer can the opening deliver? Which part still needs the countdown?

**Middle turn** — Choose the chapter where the viewer’s situation comes into focus. Give the editor a specific choice or consequence we can see or hear. What could the viewer reasonably believe before that discovery and after it?

## Conclusion

**Payoff** — Cite the result, choice or surviving evidence that answers the opening question. Return to the viewer’s situation. What can they now do, notice, feel or judge differently? If part of the question is still open, say precisely what the evidence settles.

**Last line and last image** — Write the line verbatim. Name the image or voice that carries it and how long the editor should stay with it. If an earlier object returns, say what the audience can see in it now.

## Picture and playback

Identify the few decisive visuals. For each, give the original source, the first view, the detail to reveal, and the portrait and landscape crops. Mark captions, credits and any pause needed to see or hear the change.

Where a source or diagram introduces an unfamiliar term, write the words the viewer will actually hear at its first use. Show the reader the finished audience-facing script and planned screen text. Save this treatment for comparison after the [independent script review](../references/script-reviewer.md).

After playback, record the opening question, the middle discovery and the last piece of evidence in one sentence each. Check a middle Short cold and its two joins in the film. Revise whichever sentence or cut a viewer had to guess at.
